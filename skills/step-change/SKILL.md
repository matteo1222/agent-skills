# Step Change

Use **Frame → Floor → Focus → Proof** to redesign the path to an
already-chosen outcome. Treat the compressed timeline as a design constraint
until evidence supports it as a forecast.

Read [`references/research.md`](references/research.md) when the user asks for
the framework's provenance, evidence, limitations, or application where the
downside is material.

## 1. Qualify the mission

Write down:

- the observable outcome and owner;
- the baseline and current trajectory;
- the current binding constraint;
- protected hard edges and unacceptable downside;
- counter-metrics that would expose harm or gaming;
- recent performance, available slack, and who bears the risk.

Look up discoverable facts. Label unsupported baselines, allocations, and
thresholds as unknown or proposed; do not manufacture precision.

Use this skill on one selected mission. Route unresolved competing goals through
the user's prioritization process first.

Prefer small wins and proximal learning goals when recent performance and slack
are both low, the owner cannot absorb failed experiments, or the work is novel
enough that the strategy is still unknown.

Complete this step when one outcome, its baseline, and its protected boundaries
fit in a single unambiguous statement.

## 2. Frame an impossible design horizon

Increase the outcome and compress its horizon until the current playbook
clearly fails. Use 10x, half the time, or three years only when those numbers
create the needed discontinuity; no multiplier is inherently correct.

Preserve the desired end while making scale and time force a different system.
Pair an outcome goal with a learning goal when the path is unknown.

Label the result:

- **Design horizon:** the deliberately extreme planning lens.
- **Forecast:** the date supported by present evidence.
- **Commitment:** a promise explicitly approved by its owner.

Complete the frame when linear extrapolation cannot satisfy it and the design
horizon cannot be mistaken for a forecast or promise.

## 3. Raise the floor

Inventory every material goal, task, product, channel, client, role,
assumption, and dependency in the current system. Assign each to one bucket:

- **Protect:** a safety, legal, ethical, quality, health, runway, relationship,
  or other non-negotiable floor.
- **Keep:** directly contributes to the framed outcome.
- **Redesign:** contributes, but its present mechanism cannot scale.
- **Test:** might provide nonlinear leverage, but needs evidence.
- **Park or stop:** cannot plausibly reach the frame or costs more than it
  contributes.

Expose false requirements, sunk-cost paths, vanity metrics, and lesser goals.
Derive released capacity from observed work when possible; otherwise leave it
unknown. Treat personnel, capital, production, and other consequential removals
as staged proposals requiring evidence, authority, and due process.

Complete the floor when every material current commitment has one bucket and
each proposed removal names the capacity or attention it releases.

## 4. Focus on the crux

Name the one constraint that makes the framed outcome impossible. Generate
distinct path hypotheses that change the leverage mechanism, such as:

- a simpler system or narrower offer;
- distribution through an existing audience;
- automation or a reusable asset;
- a partner with missing capability, access, or trust;
- a different business model, sequence, or target segment.

For each hypothesis, state the leverage mechanism, critical assumption, needed
people or resources, earliest signal, counter-metric, and downside. Compare them
by:

1. ability to solve the crux;
2. speed to trustworthy evidence;
3. reversibility and bounded downside; and
4. capacity released rather than hours added.

A path consisting only of more effort, more parallel work, or an unexplained
external dependency is linear. Reframe it.

Complete the focus when one hypothesis has a named leverage mechanism and wins
against the alternatives on explicit evidence and tradeoffs.

## 5. Demand proof

Turn the selected path into the smallest test that could change the decision.
State:

- the action and owner;
- the signal and pass/fail threshold;
- the shortest useful review window;
- the resource cap and counter-metrics;
- the protected boundaries;
- the work displaced;
- the expand, revise, or stop rule.

Derive thresholds from the outcome, baseline, capacity, or a stated decision
boundary. Label any remaining number as a hypothesis to calibrate.

Use proximal feedback. On complex work, test learning before demanding the
distant performance result. Schedule an alternative scan so focus does not
become overpersistence. Update the forecast only from evidence; convert it to a
commitment only with owner approval.

Complete the strategy when the next proof can run without admitting another
primary mission and its result will select among expand, revise, or stop.

## Numerical integrity gate

Audit every number before presenting the strategy. Each number must be:

- supplied by the user;
- observed from an authoritative source;
- computed from shown inputs and an equation; or
- labeled **Proposal**, with its rationale and approval status.

Replace every other number with **Unknown** and name who or what can resolve it.
Do not imply precise released capacity, resource caps, allocations, baselines,
or pass thresholds without this provenance.

Complete the gate when every numeric claim has one of the four provenances.

## Output

```markdown
## Mission
Outcome, baseline, owner, current constraint, unknowns, protective floors,
counter-metrics

## Frame
Design horizon, evidence-backed forecast, commitment status

## Floor
Protect / Keep / Redesign / Test / Park-or-stop, including released capacity

## Focus
Crux, considered paths, chosen leverage hypothesis

## Proof
Action, signal, derived threshold, resource cap, counter-metrics, review window,
displacement, decision rule
```
